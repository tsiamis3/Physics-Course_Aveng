Constants & key values

Mechanics & fluids

  • g = 32.2 ft/s² (9.81 m/s²; standard 32.174 ft/s² = 9.80665 m/s²)
  • Mass in slugs = W/32.2 (1 slug weighs 32.2 lb = 14.59 kg)
  • Water: 62.4 lb/ft³ = 1.94 slug/ft³ (1000 kg/m³) · 8.34 lb/gal · 0.433 psi per ft of depth
  • Air at sea level (ISA): ρ = 0.002377 slug/ft³ (1.225 kg/m³); at 10,000 ft 0.001756 slug/ft³
  • Standard atmosphere: 14.7 psi = 29.92 inHg = 1013 hPa (101 325 Pa) · 1 ft² = 144 in²
  • 1 lb = 4.448 N · 1 ft·lb = 1.356 J (1 J = 0.738 ft·lb)
  • 1 hp = 550 ft·lb/s = 33,000 ft·lb/min = 746 W
  • 1 US gallon = 0.1337 ft³ = 231 in³ ≈ 3.785 L
  • 1 kt = 1.688 ft/s · 1 mph = 1.467 ft/s

Heat, light & sound

  • 1 BTU = 778 ft·lb ≈ 1055 J ≈ 252 cal
  • Water: c = 1 BTU/(lb·°F) (4186 J/(kg·K)) · L_vap ≈ 970 BTU/lb (2260 kJ/kg) · L_fus ≈ 144 BTU/lb (334 kJ/kg)
  • Gas constant R = 8.314 J/(mol·K)
  • Speed of light c = 186,282 mi/s (299 792 458 m/s ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s)
  • Speed of sound, sea level 15 °C: 1,116 ft/s = 661 kt = 761 mph (340 m/s = 1,225 km/h)
  • 1 Å = 10⁻¹⁰ m = 0.1 nm · visible light 400–700 nm
  • Threshold of hearing I₀ = 10⁻¹² W/m² (0 dB)

2.01 Matter

Atomic structure

  • A = Z + N (mass number = protons + neutrons), N = A − Z
  • Neutral atom: number of electrons = Z; an ion has gained or lost electrons
  • Notation AZX, e.g. 2713Al: 13 protons, 14 neutrons, 13 electrons
  • Isotopes: same Z, different N
  • Maximum electrons in shell n: 2n² (2, 8, 18, 32)

States of matter

  • Mass density ρ = m/V · weight density γ = W/V
  • Specific gravity SG = ρ/ρ_water (no units)
  • Boiling point rises with pressure: water 100 °C at 14.7 psi, about 108 °C at 20 psi, about 90 °C at 10 psi

2.02 Mechanics

Kinematics

  • Uniform motion: x = v·t
  • Acceleration: a = (v_f − v_i)/t
  • Uniform acceleration: v = v₀ + a·t
  • x = v₀·t + ½·a·t²
  • v² = v₀² + 2·a·x
  • Free fall: a = g; from rest v = √(2·g·h)

Newton’s laws & forces

  • 1st law (inertia): ΣF = 0 ⇔ constant velocity
  • 2nd law: ΣF = m·a, with m = W/g (m in slugs, F in lb, a in ft/s²)
  • 3rd law, action–reaction: equal in size, opposite in direction
  • Weight: W = m·g
  • Thrust to accelerate: F = (W/g)·(V_f − V_i)/t

Friction

  • Static friction: equal and opposite to the applied force, up to its maximum, the limiting friction
  • Limiting (starting) friction: F = μ_s·N
  • Sliding (kinetic) friction: F = μ_k·N, a little smaller (μ = coefficient of friction, N = normal force)
  • On a level surface N = W; on a slope N = W·cos θ
  • Rolling friction is much smaller (tyre on concrete μ ≈ 0.02)

Stress & strain

  • Stress σ = F/A [psi; Pa = N/m²], same units as pressure but not the same quantity
  • Strain ε = ΔL/L (no units)
  • Hooke’s law (elastic range): σ = E·ε
  • E: steel 30 × 10⁶ psi (200 GPa), aluminium 10 × 10⁶ psi (70 GPa)
  • Types: tension, compression, shear, torsion, bending

Torque & equilibrium

  • Torque (moment): τ = F·d (d = perpendicular distance, the lever arm) [lb·ft, lb·in; N·m]
  • Equilibrium: ΣF = 0 and Στ = 0
  • Centre of gravity: CG = ΣM/ΣW (sum of moments ÷ total weight)
  • Torque wrench with extension E on a wrench of length L: reading = applied torque × L/(L + E)
  • Do not mix lb·ft (torque) with ft·lb (work)

Work, power & energy

  • Work: W = F·d (force along the displacement); in general W = F·d·cos θ [ft·lb; J]
  • Kinetic energy: KE = ½·m·v² = ½·(W/g)·v²
  • Potential energy: PE = m·g·h = W·h
  • Conservation: KE + PE = const (no friction or heat losses)
  • Power: P = Work/t = F·v = τ·ω [ft·lb/s ÷ 550 = hp; W = J/s]
  • Efficiency η = W_out/W_in = P_out/P_in (always below 100 %)

Circular & rotational motion

  • ω = 2π·n/60 (n in rpm, ω in rad/s) · v = ω·r
  • a_c = v²/r · centripetal force F_c = m·v²/r = (W/g)·v²/r, towards the centre
  • Centrifugal force: a fictitious force, needed only in the rotating frame
  • Engine: hp = τ [lb·ft] × n [rpm] ÷ 5252 (SI: kW = τ [N·m] × n [rpm] ÷ 9549)
  • At constant power, higher rpm means lower torque

Gyroscopes

  • Rigidity in space: a spinning gyro keeps its direction; it grows with mass, spin speed, mass near the rim and low bearing friction (attitude and directional indicators)
  • Precession: the gyro reacts as if the force were applied 90° ahead in the direction of rotation (turn and bank)

Oscillations

  • T = 1/f
  • Spring: F = k·x · mass on a spring: T = 2π·√(m/k)
  • Pendulum (small swings): T = 2π·√(L/g); depends on length, not on mass

Simple machines

  • A machine trades force for distance; it does not create work or energy
  • Mechanical advantage MA = F_load/F_effort · velocity ratio VR = d_effort/d_load
  • Ideal machine MA = VR · real machine η = MA/VR = W_out/W_in
  • Lever: F_e·L_e = F_l·L_l, ideal MA = L_e/L_l
  • Classes: 1st fulcrum in the middle (crowbar, pliers); 2nd load in the middle (wheelbarrow), MA > 1; 3rd effort in the middle (tweezers, landing gear), MA < 1
  • Pulleys: fixed MA = 1, movable MA = 2; block and tackle ideal MA = number of supporting rope segments
  • Gears: ratio = N_driven/N_drive = n_drive/n_driven; torque out = torque in × ratio
  • Planetary: carrier fixed, ring output: N_ring/N_sun; ring fixed, carrier output: 1 + N_ring/N_sun
  • Inclined plane: ideal MA = length/height · wedge: length/thickness
  • Screw: ideal MA = circumference/pitch = 2π·r/pitch

Momentum & collisions

  • Momentum p = m·v (a vector); m in slugs, p in slug·ft/s = lb·s
  • Impulse: F·t = m·Δv, so a longer stopping time means a smaller average force
  • Conservation (no external force): m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = const (sign the velocities)
  • Stick together: v = (m₁v₁ + m₂v₂)/(m₁ + m₂)
  • Kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic collisions
  • Jet thrust F = ṁ·Δv (slug/s × ft/s = lb)

Pressure & buoyancy

  • P = F/A [psi = lb/in²; Pa]
  • Liquid pressure: P = ρ·g·h = γ·h (water 0.433 psi per ft); depends only on depth and density
  • Absolute pressure: psia = psig + 14.7
  • Buoyancy (Archimedes): B = ρ_fluid·g·V = weight of the displaced fluid
  • Weight of a liquid: W = SG × 62.4 × V (V in ft³)

Pascal & hydraulics

  • Pascal: pressure in a confined fluid acts equally in all directions, at right angles to the walls
  • F = P·A, P = F/A, A = F/P (lb, psi, in²) · piston area A = π·d²/4
  • Hydraulic MA = F_out/F_in = A_out/A_in = d_in/d_out
  • Volume in = volume out: A_in·d_in = A_out·d_out (force gained, distance lost)
  • Real system: F_out = η × F_in × A_out/A_in

Fluid properties & flow

  • Viscosity: resistance to flow; for liquids it rises when the temperature falls
  • Liquids are almost incompressible, gases are compressible
  • Continuity: A₁·v₁ = A₂·v₂ (narrower duct, faster flow)
  • Bernoulli: p + ½ρv² + ρgh = const; where v rises, static pressure falls
  • Total pressure = static + dynamic, q = ½·ρ·v²
  • Pitot-static: q = P_total − P_static, v = √(2q/ρ)

2.03 Thermodynamics

Temperature & expansion

  • °F = 1.8·°C + 32 · °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9
  • Absolute: K = °C + 273 (273.15) · °R = °F + 460 (459.67)
  • A change of 1 °C = 1 K = 1.8 °F = 1.8 °R
  • Linear expansion: ΔL = α·L·ΔT (steel α ≈ 11 × 10⁻⁶ /°C)
  • Resistance thermometer: R = R₀(1 + α_R·ΔT), range −70 to 150 °C
  • Thermocouple: E ≈ S·(T_hot − T_cold); chromel–alumel ≈ 41 µV/°C

Heat

  • Q = m·c·ΔT (water c = 1 BTU/(lb·°F) = 4186 J/(kg·K))
  • Specific heat of a substance relative to water: c/c_water
  • Latent heat (no temperature change): Q = m·L
  • Heat transfer: conduction, convection, radiation
  • Fuel: avgas 18,900 BTU/lb; petroleum fuel ≈ 20,000 BTU/lb = 15,560,000 ft·lb

Laws & gases

  • 0th law: two bodies in equilibrium with a third are in equilibrium with each other (thermometers)
  • 1st law: Q = ΔU + W
  • 2nd law: heat flows by itself only from hot to cold; no engine converts all heat into work
  • 3rd law: absolute zero cannot be reached
  • Boyle (T const): P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ · Charles: V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂, P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂
  • General gas law: P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂ (absolute P and T)
  • Dalton: P_total = ΣPᵢ · ideal gas PV = nRT

Engines

  • Thermal efficiency η = useful work / heat from fuel
  • η = HP × 33,000 / (lb fuel per min × BTU/lb × 778) (IHP or BHP)
  • Compression ratio CR = (V_d + V_c)/V_c
  • Carnot (maximum) efficiency: η = 1 − T_c/T_h (absolute temperatures)

2.04 Optics

Light & reflection

  • c = f·λ; frequency never changes between materials, λ and speed do
  • Refractive index n = c/v ≥ 1 (air 1.00029, water 1.33, acrylic 1.49, glass 1.5–1.6, diamond 2.42)
  • Reflection: θᵢ = θᵣ, angles measured from the normal
  • Plane mirror: virtual, upright, same size, as far behind as in front
  • Spherical mirror: f = R/2 · 1/f = 1/u + 1/v · m = v/u
  • Concave converges (magnifies when close); convex diverges (always smaller)

Refraction & lenses

  • Snell: n₁·sin θ₁ = n₂·sin θ₂ (into a denser medium the ray bends towards the normal)
  • Critical angle: sin θc = n₂/n₁ (into air: water 48.8°, glass 41.8°, diamond 24.4°)
  • Apparent depth = real depth / n
  • Lens: 1/f = 1/u + 1/v · m = v/u · power P = 1/f (dioptres, f in m)
  • Convex (converging) lens: f > 0 · concave (diverging): f < 0

Fibre optics

  • Total internal reflection: sin θc = n_cladding/n_core
  • Numerical aperture NA = √(n_core² − n_cladding²)
  • Speed in fibre v = c/n (n ≈ 1.5: 200,000 km/s, 5 µs per km)
  • Link loss = dB/km × km + connectors + splices (fusion splice ≈ 0.1 dB)
  • Power ratio: −3 dB = ½, −10 dB = 1/10, −20 dB = 1/100
  • Minimum bend radius about 30 mm

2.05 Wave Motion & Sound

Waves

  • f = 1/T · v = f·λ = λ/T
  • Sine wave y = A·sin(2π·f·t); energy ∝ A²
  • Transverse: particles move at right angles to the wave · longitudinal: along it (sound in air)
  • Interference: constructive Δd = n·λ; destructive Δd = (n + ½)·λ
  • Beats: f_beat = |f₁ − f₂|
  • Standing waves: nodes ½λ apart · string / open pipe f_n = n·v/(2L) · closed pipe f_n = n·v/(4L), n odd

Sound

  • Speed of sound: solids > liquids > gases; in air it depends on temperature, not pressure
  • Air: v ≈ 1,087 + 2·T ft/s (331 + 0.6·T m/s), T in °C
  • Echo d = v·t/2 · one way (thunder) d = v·t
  • Mach number M = v/a (a = local speed of sound) · Mach cone sin μ = 1/M
  • Hearing 20 Hz – 20 kHz · pitch = frequency, loudness = amplitude
  • Blade-passing frequency = rpm/60 × number of blades

Intensity & Doppler

  • Sound level L = 10·log₁₀(I/I₀) dB
  • +3 dB = × 2 intensity (two equal sources) · +10 dB = × 10 (≈ twice as loud) · +20 dB = × 100 (≈ four times as loud)
  • Inverse square law I ∝ 1/r²: double the distance, −6 dB
  • Doppler, source approaching: f′ = f·v/(v − v_s) · receding: f′ = f·v/(v + v_s)
